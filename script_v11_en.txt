Why does a thirty pound bottle of wine look cheap on a menu? Because the one next to it costs ninety. Your brain never judges a price alone, it compares.

That's price anchoring: the first number you see becomes the ruler for everything else.

Tip one: show your premium option first. After two thousand, eight hundred feels small.

Tip two: offer three options. Most people pick the middle one.

Tip three: anchor on value. Show what the problem costs them every month before you mention your price.

Example: a designer shows a five thousand pound project first. Suddenly, the fifteen hundred pound package looks like a bargain.

One rule: the anchor must be real. Fake discounts destroy trust instantly.

This week, show your best option first, and the value before the price.
