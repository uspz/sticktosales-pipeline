Nobody wants to buy a drill. They want a hole in the wall. That is the difference between features and benefits.

A feature is what your product has. A benefit is what your customer gets out of it.

Example: "This phone has a forty-eight megapixel camera." That's a feature. Most customers don't care.

Say this instead: "You'll capture every birthday in perfect detail." That's a benefit.

Why does it work? Because people buy with emotion first, and justify it with logic later.

To turn a feature into a benefit, ask yourself: "So what?" Keep asking until you reach something the customer can feel.

A drill has a strong motor. So what? It's fast. So what? You finish the shelf today, not next weekend.

So before your next pitch, list your features. Then turn every single one into a benefit.
